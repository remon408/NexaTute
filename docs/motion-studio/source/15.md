# 15. Operations, Quality Assurance and KPIs

This chapter covers how Motion Studio is run day to day: what is measured, which quality gates every video passes, how quality is checked before changes go live, how peaks and incidents are handled, and the service levels for review and takedown queues. Targets marked "(default, editable)" are Claude-chosen starting values; they are set in the admin panel with two-person approval (see chapter 13) and reviewed after the closed pilot and the Free Beta.

## 15.1 KPIs

Events are stored in TimescaleDB and shown on Grafana dashboards; experiments run on Unleash. Each KPI is shown per package, render mode and resolution tier so that problems can be traced to their source.

| KPI | Definition | Starting target |
|---|---|---|
| First-pass approval | Share of videos the client approves without any change request | Baseline from the closed pilot (to confirm) |
| Remake rate | Share of videos sent back for a remake, split into our defects and client changes | Baseline from the closed pilot (to confirm) |
| Render success | Share of render jobs that finish without a failed or stalled stage | ≥99% (default, editable) |
| Delivery time against ETA | Actual delivery time compared with the ETA shown at order, per delivery tier | Express: within 20% of ETA; Normal ≤12 h; Slow ≤24 h |
| Quality-gate failure rate | Share of scenes failing any gate in 15.2, and share still failing after automatic repair | Thresholds from the launch benchmark |
| Trial-to-paid conversion | Free Trial and Free Beta users who buy a package | Measured in the Free Beta (to confirm) |
| Creator churn | Paid creators who do not renew in a month | Measured from the first paid month (to confirm) |
| Satisfaction | One-tap rating after each delivery | ≥4/5 (Free Beta pass condition, default, editable) |
| Emotion quality | Viewer-panel and client rating of voice emotion and matching facial expression | Set after the first panel round (to confirm) |
| Cost per output minute | GPU and CPU cost per delivered minute, by mode and tier | Margin alert below 30% (default, editable) |
| Queue wait and browser-to-server share | Wait before rendering; share of browser renders finished on the server | Watched, no fixed target |
| Review queue age | Age of the oldest item in Islamic review, safety appeals and takedown queues | Within the service levels in 15.8 |

Satisfaction loop (approved 29 October 2026): every delivery asks for a one-tap rating and "what went wrong"; the top five complaints are fixed each week; fixes are shown as "you said, we fixed" in What's new.

## 15.2 Quality gates

Every video passes automatic gates before delivery. A failed scene is repaired or re-rendered; only that scene is redone, not the whole video. Our defects are never charged.

| Gate | Check | On failure |
|---|---|---|
| Script | Safety check, fact check of claims, client approval of script and storyboard | Client edits; nothing is rendered or charged before storyboard approval |
| Motion and physics | Shots of 5 seconds or less, checked for extra limbs, sliding feet, merging objects | Scene regenerated |
| Faces and hands | Defect detection on faces and hands | Regenerated, or light face repair with GFPGAN |
| Scene check | Bent buildings, odd food, floating objects; crowd shots limited with a soft background | Scene regenerated automatically |
| Look | Colour matching, deflicker and stabilisation across scenes; skin tone kept from the reference | Automatic correction |
| Escalated upscaling | Round-trip VMAF and SSIM (libvmaf, one frame per second), Laplacian sharpness, frame-to-frame flicker; minimum bitrates 720p 5–8 Mbps, 1080p 8–12 Mbps (final from benchmark) | Re-upscale with the alternative model free; then the next native tier at the escalating price |
| Lip-sync and emotion | Automatic lip and voice sync check; facial expression matched to each line's emotion | Shot re-rendered |
| Text and captions | All on-screen text as an overlay after upscaling; Bangla shaped with HarfBuzz and tested per font; right-to-left for Urdu and Arabic | Re-composited |
| Audio | Loudness to the chosen standard (YouTube −14 LUFS, broadcast EBU R128 −23 LUFS); mastering preset; pronunciation dictionary applied | Re-mastered |
| Safety on output | Frames, audio and captions checked (see chapter 12) | Re-rendered safely or blocked |
| Final file | File exists, size and checksum valid, browser-made chunks verified, C2PA signed | Job not marked complete until it passes |

Thresholds for every gate are set from the launch benchmark and edited in the admin panel. The "2 candidates for hero shots" option adds an automatic best-pick step on Premium Starter and above (see chapter 4).

## 15.3 Viewer panel and model benchmark

- Blind viewer rating panel: before any big update (new model, new voice set, new upscaler), a panel of Bangladeshi viewers rates old and new output without knowing which is which, including emotion quality. The update goes live only if it rates at least as well (default, editable).
- Quarterly open-model benchmark: new open video, image and voice models are tested each quarter on a fixed Bangla test set for quality, GPU-minutes per output minute and licence. A winning model is switched on from the admin panel.
- Model pinning: each project stays on the model version it started with, so a series keeps its look when a model changes.
- Bangla voices: native-speaker listening test before launch; if a voice fails, the in-house VITS Bangla voices are used. New dubbing languages go live one by one after each passes its quality gate.

## 15.4 Testing before release

- Staging load test with Locust, LiveKit CLI load tests and vLLM benchmark scripts. Motion Studio is tested alone and together with NexaTute at the intended priority mix. Reports state achieved concurrency, p95/p99 latency, error rate, GPU use, queue delay and cost.
- Render-mode tests: browser rendering across the device matrix, resume after sleep or tab close, server takeover after 30 minutes, tampered-chunk detection, and server fallback at the same price.
- GPU test: one week on one rented 8× RTX A6000 server, then GPU-minutes per output minute for every mode and tier, including Cinematic 1080p Native and 4K Escalating, before rates are confirmed (see chapter 10).
- Payment tests: every test payment in the closed pilot must succeed, including refunds to the wallet.
- Every optimisation has a one-switch rollback and is logged as a model-behaviour change.

## 15.5 Festival and peak handling

Demand rises before Eid, Pohela Boishakh, 21 February, 16 December and the wedding season.

- Early-order discount for festival videos ordered ahead of the rush, and a daily capacity limit, as in NexaTute (values default, editable).
- Monthly content calendar orders render on the Slow tier at night, which spreads load away from the daytime peak.
- Express is always offered but its ETA is calculated from live load, so clients see the real wait.
- Order of measures when the pool is busy: Slow orders move to the night window, then the free daily pool shrinks, then emergency cloud GPU is used for paid work within its spending cap (order default, editable; free users never get cloud fallback).

## 15.6 Incident handling

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Render fails mid-way | Resume from the last finished scene or chunk; no charge on failure; automatic wallet refund if the video cannot be delivered |
| Browser render stops | Browser resumes after sleep or tab close; server takes over after 30 minutes or repeated failure, same price |
| GPU server down | Emergency move to rented cloud GPU within a monthly cap; affected clients get a new ETA |
| Delivery late | Express surcharge refunded automatically if more than 20% late; Normal and Slow later than 12 or 24 hours get credit |
| Platform outage | Consumed minutes or credits restored automatically within 24 hours |
| Everything unavailable | Cloudflare edge busy page in every interface language; status page shows the incident |

Security and data incidents follow the shared targets: detect within 5 minutes, contain within 60 minutes, notify within 1–4 hours as the law requires, breach notice within 72 hours, post-mortem within 72 hours of recovery. These are planning targets until restore drills prove them.

## 15.7 Support operations

Support channels, response times and the 24-hour English support model (AI first, then a person) are in chapter 9. Operations watch the support queue age and the top complaint themes, which feed the weekly fix list in 15.1.

## 15.8 Review and takedown service levels

| Queue | Service level | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Islamic content review | 3–7 days | Separate "in review" ETA; no GPU reserved or charge before approval; queue-length alert; more reviewers addable |
| Unauthorised voice clone or face use | Takedown within 24 hours | Account suspended during review |
| Brand misuse or defamation | Takedown within 24 hours | Then review |
| Copyright notice (DMCA or local) | Within 24 hours (default, editable) | Repeat-infringer rules apply |
| Child sexual content | Immediate | Account closed; reported as the law requires |
| Safety appeals and unclear cases | Within 3 working days (default, editable) | A person decides |
| Voice or twin consent withdrawal | Model deleted within 72 hours | No new renders after withdrawal |

The admin panel shows the age of every queue. Named reviewers and on-call cover for these queues are a staffing item still to be budgeted (see chapter 18).
