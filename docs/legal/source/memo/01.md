## 1.2 Applicable law

## 1.3 Our proposed position

1. Objects clause: long and specific now, because later changes need the High Court. The draft memorandum in this pack covers every activity in 1.1; regulated activities (payments, stored value, IVR, recruitment, charitable funds) only "with any approval or licence required by law"; subsidiaries abroad subject to Bangladesh Bank approval.
2. Before incorporation nothing is signed in the company's name. Owner decision (30 October 2026): the NexaTute Free Beta starts as an adult-only pilot; under-18s join only after incorporation and the Free Beta items in chapter 15. Pilot data before incorporation only with the lawyer's written position on the controller. After incorporation, the owner and every contributor assign pre-incorporation work (code, logos, content, domains) to the company in writing.
3. Vitlous account: no RemonLogic sale is collected through it. Pilot test payments use the sandbox or fully refunded test amounts (default, editable). RemonLogic applies for its own merchant account as soon as it has the certificate, trade licence, TIN, BIN and bank account; if not ready, the paid launch moves. A collection-agent fallback only with written approval from the lawyer, accountant and aamarPay.
4. USA LLC via doola.com: formation is easy, but owning and funding a foreign company from Bangladesh normally needs prior Bangladesh Bank or Government outward-investment approval, rarely given to a new startup [lawyer to confirm]. The LLC is formed and funded only after the lawyer's FX opinion and any approval. Until then RemonLogic Private Limited contracts with all customers, applies to Paddle in its own name (disclosing the AI voice and avatar features), registers the DMCA agent and asks NCMEC directly (else the paid IWF list).

## 1.4 Risks if wrong

- Narrow objects: a High Court petition. Pre-incorporation data: possible personal liability. Vitlous account: possible unlicensed aggregation, account freeze, revenue and VAT in the wrong company. USA LLC: FX breach and transfer-pricing exposure.
