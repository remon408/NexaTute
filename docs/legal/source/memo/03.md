# 3. Consumer Protection and Digital Commerce

Covers delivery, refund, renewal, terms-change, dispute and marketing promises to customers, measured against the Consumers' Right Protection Act 2009 and the Digital Commerce Operation Guidelines 2021 (DCOG), plus the EU/UK withdrawal right for consumers abroad. Applies to both products.

## 3.1 Facts

- NexaTute: monthly plans, prepay packages, Daily Pass, add-ons, learning-guarantee terms; paid from 1 January 2027. Motion Studio: packages, pre-production fee, per-minute rates at storyboard approval; Express (+20%), Normal (12 hours), Slow (24 hours); Islamic review up to 7 days. One-click cancel, proration, 7 days' notice of price rises (default, editable); app-store purchases +30%.
- Refunds: 0–30 days, 100% of the unused portion (Motion Studio package fee only if no video was made that cycle); 31+ days none; failures, outages, wrong or duplicate charges refunded; always to the original payment method (P1); late Normal or Slow videos: 100% of that order's video charge back automatically, started within 24 hours (default, editable).
- Marketing rules: no fake reviews or numbers, no scarcity wording, no "No. 1" claims, no AI testimonials. Legal pages in six languages. Owner decisions of 30 October 2026: 3.3 items 7–10.

## 3.2 Applicable law

| Law | Status and key point | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Consumers' Right Protection Act, 2009 (Act No. 26 of 2009) | In force; no 2025–26 amendment found. Offences: misleading advertisement (reported s.44), not supplying promised services (s.45), negligent service harming safety (s.53). DNCRP complaints within 30 days (s.60); fines partly paid to complainants | Medium; low on sections |
| DCOG 2021 (Ministry of Commerce, 4 July 2021, amended 2021) | Operative as far as found; covers goods and services sold online. Display trade licence, VAT number, TIN, DBID; refund, return and delivery terms in Bangla; checkbox consent before collecting data | Medium; SaaS applicability not confirmed |
| DCOG — delivery and refunds | Hand-over within 48 hours; delivery within 5 or 10 days. For delay after advance payment, full refund within 10 days through the same payment channel. If the seller cannot supply: notice within 48 hours, refund within 72 hours of the notice | Medium |
| DCOG — other | 100% advance only for ready-to-ship goods or with an approved escrow; complaint reply within 72 hours; a compliance officer liaising with the DNCRP; reviews not deleted; records kept 6 years | Medium |

## 3.3 Our proposed position

1. Treat the DCOG as applying to both products until the lawyer says otherwise.
2. Before paid launch, show on both websites the company name, address, trade licence, BIN, TIN, DBID (if required), compliance officer and contact addresses [to insert]. Each type of work (support, billing, disputes, licence checks, copyright, privacy, legal requests and others) has its own email address, set in the admin panel and routed to the owning team.
3. Publish refund, cancellation, delivery and complaint terms in Bangla and English; for consumers, both versions are official and the one more favourable to the consumer applies (default, editable) [lawyer to confirm]. English may prevail for business terms.
4. Every refund to the original payment method within 10 days (default, editable); no credit, no set-off; refunds below ৳20 batched monthly only if the lawyer agrees. Delivery promises stay, with the automatic refunds in 3.1.
5. A written basis for every public claim; public reviews never deleted except unlawful content.
6. A plan paid at the start of a service that starts at once is not, in Claude's reading, a restricted "advance payment" [lawyer to confirm].
7. Disputes: AI support first (it may correct payment or order errors at once); then email to a person who reviews the record and decides, with a first reply in 72 hours and a decision in 10 working days (default, editable); DNCRP or court remain open. The AI never decides a dispute, refund refusal or account action. Records kept 6 years.
8. Auto-renewal only by separate consent, never pre-selected, with reminders 3 days (monthly) and 30 days (annual) before (default, editable). Terms are versioned; a material change gets 30 days' notice and needs active re-acceptance; until then the customer may cancel pro rata but place no new paid orders. No "continued use means acceptance".
9. EU/UK consumers: 14-day withdrawal right; at checkout they request immediate start and accept paying for what was provided; a delivered video order ends the right for that order [international firm to confirm].
10. Regulated businesses (recruiting agencies, clinics, real estate): automatic format check and a registry lookup where an official source exists; human review for high-risk sectors; never shown as "verified"; a failed check disputed by email to a person; the client alone responsible for its documents and claims.

## 3.4 Risks if wrong

- DNCRP fines; English-only terms unenforceable; deemed acceptance of changed terms unenforceable; liability for a client's false licence; chargebacks and settlement holds.

## 3.5 Questions for the lawyer

1. Does the DCOG bind an online-education and AI-video SaaS provider, and the free Free Beta?
2. Must every refund go back through the same channel within 10 days, and may small refunds be batched monthly?
3. Do the delivery rules apply to instant digital services? Is a plan paid at the start a restricted "advance payment"?
4. Must consumer terms be in Bangla, and may English prevail (or the more favourable version)?
5. Is DBID required? Does the draft Cross-Border Digital Commerce Policy 2026 affect Paddle sales?
6. Are the refund rules in 3.1 lawful ("no refund after 30 days"; "package fee only if no video was made")?
7. Which AI marketing statements are risky ("AI teacher", "learning guarantee", "Quality Checked")?
8. Must the compliance officer be named to the DNCRP? Does the dispute route meet the 72-hour rule? Are 3.3 items 8 and 10 enforceable under the CRPA 2009?
