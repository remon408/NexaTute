# 13. Employment, Contractors and Performers

Covers employees (including night-shift support), contractors, voice artists, actors and presenters, photographers, reviewers, teachers and counsellors, and programme partners. Employment law was not in the research note; every legal statement here is [lawyer to confirm].

## 13.3 Our proposed position

1. Employees: written appointment letters from day one covering probation, hours, pay, leave, notice, confidentiality, data protection, acceptable use and assignment of all work-related IP; a staff privacy notice explaining monitoring of work systems.
2. Night shift: voluntary with written consent; transport or allowance after a set hour (default, editable); shift or overtime pay as the law requires; any special rule for women applied exactly as the lawyer advises.
3. Contractors: written service agreements with scope, fees and tax deduction, full assignment of rights in deliverables, confidentiality, data-processing terms and data return or deletion; avoid treating long-term full-time contractors as employees without employment terms.
4. Performer agreements (voice artists, actors, presenters), signed before every session:
  - consent to record, clone and synthesise, with scope stated specifically (products, languages, emotions, child-character styling, derived voices, licence to clients, stock sale);
  - duration; payment (fixed fee and any royalty set by the owner);
  - withdrawal with 30 days' notice (default, editable): no new renders afterwards, delivered videos remain;
  - prohibited uses (political, sexual, defamatory, scam, religious misrepresentation);
  - performer-rights and moral-rights consents as far as the law allows;
  - the right to independent advice; age 18+ with identity checked; a privacy notice for voice and face data.
5. Reviewers: contracts with confidentiality, conflict-of-interest clauses and indemnity for good-faith decisions; no reviewer views known child sexual abuse material (handled by hash matching and reporting); exposure limits and wellbeing support (default, editable).
6. Teachers, mentors and counsellors: per-session agreements with safeguarding code, no off-platform contact with learners, data rules and quality standards; counsellors give no clinical treatment unless qualified [lawyer to confirm any licence].
7. Job assignment: each queue and approval has a named owner and deputy; only staff whose contract covers the role are assigned; the AI Co-Admin never approves; a logged founder-unavailable route for child-safety or fraud emergencies (default, editable).
8. Resellers, affiliates, marketplace sellers and the creator challenge: written programme terms (commission, tax, prohibited claims, brand use, data, termination); no paid entry to challenges.

## 13.4 Risks if wrong

- Labour claims and inspections; contractor work that stays the contractor's property; a voice artist disputing AI use, forcing library voices out of client projects; reviewer harm; night-shift safety incidents.

## 13.5 Questions for the lawyer

1. Does the Labour Act 2006 apply to office staff, and which roles are excluded?
2. What rules apply to night shifts, especially for women? What must an appointment letter contain, and what probation is lawful?
3. What is the test for treating a contractor as an employee?
4. Are performers' rights and moral rights waivable for AI voice and likeness use?
5. Do counsellors speaking with guardians about a child's wellbeing need a licence?
6. What rules apply to prize competitions such as the creator challenge?
7. May an outsourced or part-time trust-and-safety on-call service handle night queues, and on what contract and data terms?
