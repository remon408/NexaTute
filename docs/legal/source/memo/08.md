# 8. Children

Rules on children in NexaTute, PlayBoom and Motion Studio (data-law points in chapters 6 and 7).

## 8.1 Facts

- Many NexaTute learners are under 18; PlayBoom serves young children. Free Betas include children (NexaTute from 20 November 2026; PlayBoom 1–20 January 2027).
- Age check: date of birth plus guardian OTP for under-18s; the guardian decides every consent switch.
- PlayBoom: guardian account with child profiles and PIN; no face templates; only the guardian buys; the AI Play Buddy says it is an AI.
- NexaTute minors: face verification with guardian consent; no private adult–child chat; no ads or trackers; batch recordings (when one member asks) include other learners' question audio; app-store purchases only with guardian approval.
- Live Quran teachers, mentors and institution teachers may meet children.
- Motion Studio: child-character voices only from paid adult voice artists or synthetic voices; an adult's own clone may sound child-like; never a real child's voice, even with guardian consent. Child characters trigger kids-safe strict filters; kids-safe mode sets YouTube "Made for kids". Cloning and twins are 18+. Zero tolerance for child sexual content, with hash matching and immediate closure.
- A NexaTute "making videos with AI" course leads to a Motion Studio trial; Motion Studio's minimum age is not stated.

## 8.2 Applicable law

| Law | Status and key point | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Children Act, 2013 (Act No. 24 of 2013), s.4 | In force. A child is under 18. Mainly protection and justice; no online-service duty found; protections on identifying children may matter for recordings and showcases | High / medium |
| Majority Act, 1875; Contract Act, 1872, s.11 | Majority at 18; a minor's agreement is void (Mohori Bibee) | High |
| Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 | Guardian consent; no tracking, profiling or targeted ads directed at children (chapter 6) | Medium |
| Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 / Act 2026 | Offences for AI-generated child sexual abuse material and sextortion (chapter 9) | High (2025 text) |
| COPPA | US under-13s (chapter 7) | High |

## 8.3 Our proposed position

1. The guardian is the contracting party and buyer for every under-18 learner; the child is a profile under the guardian's account. A short child-friendly explanation is shown to the learner (assent, not consent).
2. Guardian consent per feature (learning, recordings, face verification, anonymised AI training, institution sharing, MindForge sharing), recorded with version and time. OTP plus an adult declaration unless the lawyer requires stronger verification.
3. Institutions confirm in their agreement that they hold guardian consent and produce it on request.
4. No profiling, engagement intelligence or personalised or cross-product offers for under-18 learners; face verification off for them (chapter 6). The "making videos with AI" course shows the Motion Studio offer only to learners aged 18 or over (default, editable).
5. Batch recordings: learners whose guardians have not consented are not captured (audio muted, name hidden) (default, editable).
6. Staff and teachers with access to children: safeguarding code, identity and reference checks, police verification where lawful [lawyer to confirm], logged sessions, no off-platform contact.
7. Showcases and leaderboards show first name and initial only unless the guardian agrees (default, editable).
8. Motion Studio accounts are 18+ only (default, editable).
9. Real children in Motion Studio uploads: only in the client's own real footage, with the client's written warranty of guardian consent; real children's faces or voices are never animated, cloned, twinned or altered by AI (default, editable) [lawyer to confirm].
10. Child sexual abuse material: blocked by hash matching and classifiers; account closed; evidence preserved without staff viewing; reported to the authority the lawyer names; no notice to the account holder.

## 8.4 Risks if wrong

- Void contracts with minors (refunds, unenforceable terms); children's-data offences.
- Harm to a child through staff or users; AI sexual content involving a child if controls fail; family claims over a real child's likeness.
