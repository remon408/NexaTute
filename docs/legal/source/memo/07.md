# 7. International Privacy — EU, UK and USA

Covers the GDPR and UK GDPR (including the Article 27 representative), California's CCPA/CPRA and the US COPPA. For the international law firm the owner approved before EU and US marketing (30 October 2026).

## 7.1 Facts

- NexaTute and PlayBoom are sold in USD to learners abroad; Motion Studio in USD, EUR, GBP and other currencies, with an enterprise DPA and later EU data residency. Data is stored in the USA (possibly the Netherlands).
- Owner decisions (30 October 2026): EU and UK GDPR representatives; DPO question for the international lawyer; DMCA agent and CCPA; international review before EU/US marketing. MindForge assessment is off in the EU.
- Special-category data: voice prints and face data; children's data.

## 7.2 Applicable law

| Law | Status and key point | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| GDPR Art. 3(2) and Art. 27 | In force. A non-EU controller offering services to, or monitoring, people in the EU must designate in writing an EU representative. The exemption (occasional, low-risk processing) does not fit a continuously marketed SaaS processing voiceprints, face data (Art. 9) and children's data. Paddle as merchant of record does not remove the duty | High |
| UK GDPR Art. 27 | In force; unchanged by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025; only the regulator's name changed on 30 September 2026 (S.I. 2026/386) | High |
| CCPA/CPRA | In force. Applies if any one is met: worldwide revenue above $26,625,000; data of 100,000+ California consumers bought, sold or shared a year; 50%+ of revenue from selling or sharing data | High |
| COPPA and the amended Rule | Full compliance from 22 April 2026. Applies to child-directed services or actual knowledge of US under-13 users, whatever the operator's country. Separate parental consent for third-party disclosure (reportedly including targeted ads and AI training); written retention policy and security programme; vendor assurances; voiceprints and faceprints covered; up to $53,088 per violation | High |
| GDPR Art. 8 (consent age 13–16), Art. 37 (DPO), transfer rules; other US state laws | General knowledge or not researched | [international lawyer to confirm] |

## 7.3 Our proposed position

1. GDPR and UK GDPR apply from the first EU or UK user. Appoint EU and UK representatives before EU or UK marketing and name them in the Privacy Policies [to insert].
2. Decide with the international firm whether a GDPR DPO is required (likely, given biometric and children's data) and whether the Bangladesh DPO can act.
3. Lawful bases: contract; explicit consent for voice and face; parental consent below each member state's age. Transfers under standard contractual clauses or equivalent. No profiling of EU children; MindForge assessment off.
4. CCPA: almost certainly not covered at launch. Publish a short voluntary notice for California residents without claiming compliance; track the three thresholds with an alert at 50% (default, editable); never sell or share personal data for cross-context advertising.
5. COPPA: block US under-13 users of NexaTute and PlayBoom (age check asks country and date of birth) until a COPPA programme exists: FTC-recognised parental consent, written retention policy, written security programme, vendor assurances, separate consent for any third-party disclosure, no AI training on children's data (default, editable).
6. Motion Studio is not child-directed; under-18s use it only as a profile under a guardian's account, and US under-13s are blocked (chapter 8).
7. EU and UK consumers get the 14-day withdrawal right with the immediate-start acknowledgement (chapter 3).

## 7.4 Risks if wrong

- GDPR/UK GDPR fines, processing bans and transfer suspensions; COPPA penalties (a date-of-birth question creates actual knowledge); misleading-advertising exposure for unfounded compliance claims.
