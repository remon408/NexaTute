# 5. Foreign Exchange

Covers money received from foreign customers (aamarPay international cards now, Paddle later), payments for foreign servers and software, and the foreign-exchange side of the USA LLC question.

## 5.1 Facts

- Foreign customers pay through aamarPay international cards now and through Paddle later (USD, EUR, GBP; INR, PKR, AED and SAR prices approved). Paddle's payout method to Bangladesh is not confirmed.
- Recurring foreign costs: US servers, a possible Dutch GPU server, Cloudflare, cloud GPU, AI APIs (NexaLogicDev), the C2PA certificate, domains, email, SMS, stock media, Paddle fees.

## 5.2 Applicable law

Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947, Bangladesh Bank's Guidelines for Foreign Exchange Transactions, and: FEPD-1 Circular No. 40 (30 September 2026; master circular on outward current-account remittances, valid 1 year); FEPD-1 Circular No. 22 (22 July 2026; freelancers and individual exporters); FE Circular No. 31 (31 July 2025; OPGSP, MFS and PSP channels and ERQ). Confidence high on the circulars' text, medium on their application to Paddle.

- Service-export proceeds must be repatriated within the goods-export time limits; keeping proceeds abroad in any form breaches the Act, except approved notional or merchant accounts.
- Inward remittances above USD 20,000 need an online Form-C. Approved OPGSPs may carry small proceeds (up to USD 10,000 per transaction).
- ERQ: up to 50% of net software and ICT earnings may be kept in foreign currency and used for software, domain, hosting and server costs.
- Circular 40, Part-E paragraph 11: banks may remit cloud, IT infrastructure, software support and remote-application fees with no stated annual cap if there is a licensed product and valid agreement, an invoice per payment, an undertaking to use one bank and bring funds back if the service is not received, tax and VAT at source, and for later payments a certificate of satisfactory use.
- Individuals' virtual cards are capped at USD 500 a year. Corporate card rules (Part-J) were not read. Residents owning a foreign company was not researched [lawyer to confirm].

## 5.3 Our proposed position

1. One authorised dealer bank for the company, for export receipts and outward remittances; ERQ account opened.
2. Paddle payouts go straight to the company account as export receipts, with Form-C where required; no Paddle balance is kept beyond its normal payout cycle [lawyer to confirm this is not "keeping proceeds abroad"].
3. Foreign suppliers: agreement in the company's name (online terms saved as PDF), invoice per payment, Circular 40 undertaking; bank deducts VAT and tax.
4. No company bill paid from personal cards after incorporation; earlier personal payments recorded and reimbursed only as the accountant and bank allow.
5. The USA LLC (via doola.com) is formed and funded only after the lawyer's FX opinion and any Bangladesh Bank or Government outward-investment approval; meanwhile sales go through Paddle in RemonLogic's own name (chapter 1).

## 5.4 Risks if wrong

- Proceeds kept abroad or repatriated late: breach of the Act, penalties, refused remittances.
- Foreign bills not meeting Circular 40: remittance refused; servers cut off for non-payment.
- Personal cards: USD 500 cap exceeded; costs not deductible.
- LLC without approval: FX breach; its receipts treated as proceeds kept abroad.
