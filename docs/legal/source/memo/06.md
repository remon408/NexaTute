# 6. Data Protection — Bangladesh

Covers the Bangladesh data law for both products: consent, children, biometric data, transfers, relocation orders, rights and breaches (children's product rules in chapter 8; EU, UK and US in chapter 7).

## 6.1 Facts

- Servers in the USA, possibly a GPU server in the Netherlands; Cloudflare R2 and CDN. Processors: hosting, Cloudflare, email, SMS, IVR, aamarPay, Paddle, app stores; outside AI APIs for NexaLogicDev only. Learner data and children's sessions never go to outside AI; spot cloud GPU never receives voice samples or personal data.
- Data held: identity and guardian phone; learning records, recordings by choice, handwriting photos; engagement-intelligence logs (answer time, pauses, interest map, drop-out prediction); NexaTute face templates; Motion Studio voice samples, voice models, twin videos and consent recordings (separate encrypted store, no staff playback); client uploads and clients' customer lists; payment records (no card numbers).
- Adopted: a switch per feature; guardian decides for under-18s; under-18 profiling and offers locked off until the lawyer confirms (26 October 2026).
- Retention: NexaTute records 5 years after last use; voice, images and handwriting 90 days after the plan; recordings 12 months; consent records 3 years; likeness models deleted within 72 hours of withdrawal; billing 7 years.

## 6.2 Applicable law

| Law | Status | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (from Ordinance No. 61 of 2025 and Amendment Ordinance No. 23 of 2026) | Reported in force from 15 April 2026; cited as Law 63 of 2026 by one source; Gazette text not seen; a phase-in to about May 2027 not confirmed | Medium (in force); low (number, dates, localisation, penalties) [lawyer to confirm] |
| National Data Management Act, 2026 | Data authority under the Prime Minister's Office | Medium |

Key points reported (secondary sources; check against the Act):

- Extraterritorial: covers processing abroad for offering services to, or monitoring, people in Bangladesh.
- Consent free, specific, explicit, revocable and provable. Sensitive data (including biometric) needs specific consent plus another basis; voice prints and face data are likely biometric.
- Children (under 18): guardian consent before processing; tracking, monitoring, profiling and targeted advertising directed at children prohibited; misuse of children's data is an offence.
- Rights: access, portability, correction (30 days), erasure, withdrawal, limits on automated decisions.
- Localisation: the 2025 text kept confidential and restricted data in Bangladesh; a trade association reports the 2026 revision removed this but kept Article 29(7) (relocation orders within 60 days) and 29(5) (fees on profits from citizens' data). Not confirmed.
- Transfers: with consent, by contract, or for education with consent, to destinations with safeguards; large-scale sensitive transfers need permission or notice.
- Significant breaches notified to the authority (deadline by regulation); "significant" fiduciaries appoint a Chief Data Officer; penalties differ by version (Tk 25–50 lakh, or up to 1–5% of turnover in the Ordinance).

## 6.3 Our proposed position

1. Comply now as if fully in force. RemonLogic is the data fiduciary; every provider signs a DPA forbidding its own use; a DPO is appointed before Free Beta [to insert].
2. Layered notices in Bangla and English; one switch per purpose; no pre-ticked boxes; consent records with version and time.
3. Children: guardian consent first; no tracking, profiling or targeted advertising for under-18 learners, even with guardian consent, until the lawyer confirms. Engagement intelligence (learner state, interest map, drop-out prediction, Shorts ranking, individual A/B tests, reminder timing) stays off; only non-profiling adaptive teaching (answers and mastery inside the syllabus). No personalised or cross-course offers to under-18s; guardian offers generic (default, editable).
4. Voice prints, face templates and twins treated as biometric: separate consent, the contract as second basis, a DPIA, encrypted storage. NexaTute face verification off for under-18s (default, editable).
5. Transfers: consent, contract and safeguards; the Privacy Policy names countries and providers; a plan can move children's data to a Bangladesh store within 60 days of an order.
6. Self-service export and deletion; correction within 30 days; human review of significant automated decisions; breach notice to the authority and users within 72 hours (default, editable).
7. Motion Studio customer lists: client is controller, RemonLogic processor; lists deleted 30 days after the campaign (default, editable).

## 6.4 Risks if wrong

- Children's-data offences (engagement intelligence is the largest risk); invalid biometric consent; a 60-day relocation order; consents collected before incorporation may be invalid.
