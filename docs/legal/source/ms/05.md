We respect the rights of creators and expect our users to do the same. This policy explains how to report content that infringes your copyright or trade mark, how a user can respond, how fast we act, and what happens to users who infringe repeatedly. It also gives practical guidance on music and platform rules.

## 5.1 What we do to prevent infringement

5.1.1 Before we process an uploaded video (for example to make Shorts, translate or edit it), the user declares that they own it or have the right to use it. We store the declaration with the time, the account and a fingerprint of the file.

5.1.2 Importing from the user's own connected YouTube or Facebook channel is the preferred way to show ownership.

5.1.3 Uploaded audio is checked against our music library and a list of known copyrighted sources, and matches are blocked or flagged. Background music can be replaced with library or our own AI music. Third-party logos detected in a video trigger a warning.

5.1.4 These checks reduce the risk; they do not remove it. We do not have a database as large as YouTube's Content ID, and we do not promise that any video is free of third-party rights.

## 5.2 How to report infringement (notice)

5.2.1 Anyone who believes content made, stored or published through Motion Studio infringes their copyright can send a notice. You do not need an account.

5.2.2 Send your notice by email to the Copyright and takedown email on our Contact page [set in admin: Copyright & takedown email], by post to [to insert address], or through the Report button on the video. Notices under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) may also be sent to our designated agent in 5.4.

5.2.3 Your notice must include:

1. Your name, address, phone number and email address.
2. A description of the copyrighted work you say is infringed (or, for many works, a representative list).
3. Where the infringing content is (a link, the video's ID, or enough detail for us to find it).
4. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
5. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or authorised to act for the owner.
6. Your physical or electronic signature.

5.2.4 Items 4 and 5 follow the DMCA (17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3)); we accept such notices from any country.

5.2.5 Bangladesh route. Rights holders in Bangladesh may send the same notice under the Copyright Act 2023, in Bangla or English [lawyer to confirm the Act number and any statutory notice requirements]. We handle it in the same way and on the same timeline.

5.2.6 Do not send a false notice. Under the DMCA, a person who knowingly misrepresents that content is infringing may be liable for damages (§512(f)), and false notices may also be unlawful in Bangladesh.

## 5.3 What we do with a notice

5.3.1 We acknowledge a complete notice and remove or disable the content within 24 hours. Removal covers storage in the Service, shared links, review links and our own pages. We also stop publishing it through our connections.

5.3.2 We tell the user, give them a copy of the notice (we may remove the complainant's contact details unless the law requires them) and explain how to send a counter-notice. We cannot remove copies on other platforms, but we tell the complainant where we know the content was published through our connections.

5.3.3 If a notice is incomplete, we ask for the missing information before acting, except in urgent cases. We keep a record of every notice, counter-notice and action for the repeat-infringer rule in 5.6, for 3 years after the account's last strike (default, editable) [lawyer to confirm].

## 5.5 Counter-notice

5.5.1 If your content was removed and you believe this was a mistake or that you had the right to use the material, you can send a counter-notice to the same address.

5.5.2 Your counter-notice must include:

1. Your name, address, phone number and email address.
2. The content that was removed and where it was before removal.
3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith that the content was removed by mistake or misidentification.
4. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal court for the district of your address or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which RemonLogic may be found, and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the notice or their agent (DMCA §512(g)(3)) [lawyer to confirm the equivalent wording for the Bangladesh route, for example consent to the courts of Dhaka].
5. Your physical or electronic signature.

5.5.3 We send the counter-notice to the person who sent the original notice. If they do not tell us within 10 working days that they have started court action, we restore the content between 10 and 14 working days after we received the counter-notice. We do not decide who is right; if the parties go to court, we follow the court's order.

5.7.1 Trade-mark owners can report misuse of a mark or brand as in 5.2, naming the mark and its registration. Brand-misuse and defamation complaints lead to takedown within 24 hours, then review; content is restored if the complaint is not upheld.

## 5.8 Protecting your own videos

5.8.1 Every Motion Studio video carries an invisible re-upload watermark. If someone re-uploads your video without permission, report it from your account and we provide an evidence report (watermark result and Content Credentials) for your complaint to that platform. Our public verification tool can confirm whether a video was made with Remon Motion Studio by reading our own watermark and Content Credentials. It is not a general AI-detection tool.

## 5.10 Platform rules warning and contact

5.10.1 Platform reuse rules are often stricter than copyright law. YouTube and Facebook restrict monetisation of reused and of mass-produced or repetitive ("inauthentic") content; Shorts, compilations and translations of other people's videos may count as reused content even with a licence; and changing the voice or remaking a video does not by itself make it original. We warn you at order and before publishing, but compliance with platform rules is your responsibility, and we do not promise monetisation.
