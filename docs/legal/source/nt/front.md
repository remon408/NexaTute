## 0.1 Purpose of this pack

This pack contains the public legal pages for NexaTute (nexatute.com), the AI tutoring platform of RemonLogic Private Limited. The pages are written for parents, guardians and students: plain English, numbered clauses, and a short summary box at the top of each document. The English version prevails. Each page will also be shown in Bangla, Hindi, Urdu, Arabic and French, and the consent screen shows a 3–4 line plain-language summary in the user's language.

The drafts follow the product decisions recorded in the Master Blueprint (sections 3, 11, 19 and 36) and the owner's decision log up to 30 October 2026, and the legal research note of 30 October 2026. Where the research could not confirm a legal point, the draft says "[lawyer to confirm]".

## 0.5 Points for the lawyer (priority order)

1. **Prepaid wallet.** The Bangladesh Bank "Guidelines for issuing Prepaid Instrument (PI) by non-payment system entities, 2024" appear to cover a closed wallet, vouchers and gift cards usable only for the issuer's own services. Approval needs 5 years of business and Tk 20 crore paid-up capital, which a new company cannot meet. Clause 1.7 of the Terms is therefore drafted in two versions: Option A (prepaid learning wallet) and Option B (plans and packs bought per period, no stored money balance). The lawyer chooses one. The same question applies to Shikkha Salami gift cards, wallet credits given for outages and the learning guarantee, referral and bonus minutes, and the Sponsor-a-Learner wallet.
2. **Payment collection through Vitlous Technology's aamarPay account.** Whether this is allowed under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2024, the aggregator rules and aamarPay's terms, and what the receipt and the Terms must say.
3. **Personal Data Protection Act 2026.** The owner follows the draft legal opinion: Act No. 63 of 2026, section 29 on cross-border transfers, NexaTute as data fiduciary. The research could not verify the Act number, the commencement dates, whether the localisation rule survived, or the penalties. Please confirm all citations, including the Cyber Security Act 2026 (Act 81) and the National Data Management Act 2026 (Act 80).
4. **Children: no profiling, tracking or targeted advertising.** The drafts say that learning analytics for under-18s are limited to teaching, that the engagement and personalisation switches stay locked off, and that cross-product offers and advertising are never shown to under-18s. Please confirm that adaptive teaching based on answer correctness and mastery is permitted, and whether the Help-button purchase prompts (Daily Pass, plan) seen by under-18 learners must also be removed or shown only to the guardian. The draft currently says offers to under-18s are neutral, never based on profiling, and always need guardian approval.
5. **DCOG 2021.** Whether it binds NexaTute as a digital service. If so: (a) terms on refund, return and delivery must be shown in Bangla, which conflicts with "English prevails"; (b) the trade licence, VAT, TIN and DBID must be displayed, but the owner decided the website will not show the trade licence; (c) refund within 10 days through the same payment channel; (d) a complaint reply within 72 hours; (e) records kept 6 years.
6. **Contracts with minors.** Under the Contract Act 1872 s.11 a minor's agreement is void, so the drafts make the guardian the contracting party and payer for every learner under 18. Please confirm the wording, and what happens when a learner turns 18.
7. **Cross-border transfers to the USA (and possibly the Netherlands).** Confirm the safeguards needed under the PDPA, the GDPR (standard contractual clauses) and the UK GDPR; and whether children's data must be stored in Bangladesh (the blueprint fallback in 36.2).
8. **Cloud AI fallback.** The legal draft (36.1) says children's sessions are never sent to cloud AI. The resource plan (8.x) says minors' sessions go to cloud AI only through compliant providers, otherwise they queue. The drafts follow the stricter rule (never for under-18s). The owner should confirm, and the lawyer should check the provider terms (zero retention, no training).
9. **Retention conflicts.** Learning records: 5 years after last use (decided 26 October 2026) against "while active plus 12 months" in 36.1. Adult proctoring snapshots: 6 months (3.27) against 30 days (Appendix G). Personal storage files: 30 days after a plan ends (29 October 2026) against 90 days for voice, images and handwriting (36.1). The drafts use the newest decision in each case and flag the others.
10. **Wrong-answer credit rule.** Clause 1.9.4 of the Terms adds a proposed credit for 1:1 minutes lost to a confirmed wrong AI answer. This is a new business rule (default, editable) for the owner to approve.
11. **Refund timelines.** 11.6 gives 100% of the unused portion within 30 days and nothing after 30 days. Check against the Consumers' Right Protection Act 2009 and the DCOG, and confirm the refund channel and processing time (the draft says 10 working days).
12. **COPPA.** US learners under 13 are put on a waiting list until verifiable parental consent is built. The COPPA section describes the rule for when it opens. Confirm.
13. **CCPA.** RemonLogic is expected to be below all three CCPA thresholds. The draft gives a short California notice. Confirm whether it is needed at all.
14. **EU AI Act.** Article 50(1) disclosure for the AI tutor has applied since 2 August 2026; MindForge assessment is off in the EU. Confirm that the log-based "stuck / didn't understand" signals are not emotion recognition.
15. **Liability cap** (Terms 1.18): 12 months' fees (default, editable). Confirm that it is enforceable against consumers in Bangladesh and abroad.
16. **Governing law and courts:** Bangladesh law, Dhaka courts. Confirm, and confirm the wording that keeps the mandatory rights of consumers abroad.
17. **No liability for centre-uploaded and approved content** (Cambridge, Pearson and IB papers uploaded by a school; teacher-, guardian- or school-approved content). The owner wants this stated; the lawyer finalises the wording.
18. **Privacy non-interference statement.** The owner wants the pages to say that NexaTute does not interfere with the privacy of students, parents, teachers or schools and only filters bad language, pornography and similar harmful content (Nexa Guard). The draft includes it; the lawyer checks it against the monitoring that does take place (safety alerts, link checks, chat compliance checks).
