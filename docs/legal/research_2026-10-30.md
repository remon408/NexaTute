- **Prepared:** 30 October 2026, from web sources available on that date.
- **For:** a Bangladeshi lawyer to check. This is **not legal advice**. It reports what the sources say.
- **Scope:** NexaTute (AI tutoring, includes under-18s, guardian consent, recordings, prepaid learning wallet) and Remon Motion Studio (AI video SaaS: voice cloning with consent, digital twins, avatars, publishing to YouTube/Meta/TikTok). Sold in Bangladesh (bKash/Nagad/cards through aamarPay; the merchant account is currently in Vitlous Technology) and abroad (Paddle once approved). Servers in the USA, possibly the Netherlands. **How to read this note**
- **Confidence:** *high* means an official or primary text was read. *medium* means reputable secondary sources agree, but the primary text was not read. *low* means one source, conflicting sources, or general knowledge.
- **Primary texts:** Bangladeshi Acts passed in April 2026 (the ordinance-to-Act conversions) were **not** found on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd during this research. Their numbers and section numbers must be checked against the Bangladesh Gazette.

## Bangladesh

### 1. Personal data protection

**Status:** **In force as an Act, reported but not verified against the Gazette.**
- **Personal Data Protection Ordinance, 2025 (Ordinance No. 61 of 2025):** promulgated 6 Nov 2025 alongside the National Data Governance Ordinance, 2025.
- **Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 (Ordinance No. 23 of 2026):** promulgated 5 Feb 2026. It changed the data-residency rules.
- **Conversion to an Act:** Parliament then passed it. The Daily Star (7 Jul 2026) says it "became the Personal Data Protection Act on April 15, 2026". Securiti cites it as "Personal Data Protection Act, 2026, Law 63 of 2026". The Act number is **unverified**.
- **Regulator:** the companion National Data Management Bill, 2026 passed on 10 Apr 2026. It sets up the regulator, a data authority under the Prime Minister's Office. Sources name it variously as the National Data Management/Governance Authority or the Data Protection Authority.
- **Commencement:** one vendor source says some provisions are delayed by about 18 months (to around May 2027). This is **not confirmed**.

**Key points (from secondary sources; check against the Act text):**
- **Scope is extraterritorial.** The law covers processing outside Bangladesh for offering goods or services to people in Bangladesh, or for monitoring them. It would reach both products even with US/NL servers.
- **Consent** must be free, specific, explicit/unambiguous and revocable. The controller ("data fiduciary") must prove consent. Notices must state the purpose, retention period, third-party transfers and how to withdraw consent.
- **Sensitive data** (financial, health, biometric, genetic) needs specific consent plus another legal basis.
  - Voice prints and face data used for voice cloning and digital twins are likely to be **biometric** data.
- **Children:**
  - A child is under 18 (Securiti; the government may set a different age).
  - **Parent or guardian consent** is required before processing a child's data.
  - Tracking, monitoring, profiling and **targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited** (Prothom Alo, Mahbub & Co). This is relevant to NexaTute's engagement analytics and marketing.
  - Misuse of children's data is a specific offence.
- **Data subject rights:** access, portability, correction (within 30 days), erasure, withdrawal of consent, and restriction of automated decisions.
- **Classification and localisation:**
  - The 2025 Ordinance classified data as public, internal, confidential or restricted. It required **confidential and restricted data to be stored in Bangladesh**.
  - CCIA (Apr 2026) says the 2026 revision **removed the localisation mandate and criminal liability for officers**. However, Art. 29(7) still lets the Authority order cloud infrastructure to be reconfigured or relocated within 60 days. Art. 29(5) lets the government levy fees on profits from Bangladeshi citizens' data.
  - Cross-border transfer of classified data is allowed with consent, under a contract with the data subject, or for education-related interests with consent. The destination must have prescribed safeguards. Large-scale transfers of sensitive identifiers need prior permission or notice to the Authority.
  - Whether the enacted Act matches the CCIA "revised text" is **not confirmed**.
- **Breaches** likely to cause significant harm must be notified to the Authority. The deadline will be set by regulations.
- **Officers:** "significant" fiduciaries must appoint a Chief Data Officer.
- **Penalties (figures differ between versions):**
  - Ordinance: 1–2% of turnover (2–5% for significant fiduciaries), and up to 5–7 years' imprisonment or Tk 20 lakh.
  - Act per Securiti: fines up to Tk 25 lakh, or Tk 50 lakh for significant fiduciaries.

### 2. Cyber security law

**Status:**
- **Cyber Security Act 2023:** repealed by the **Cyber Security Ordinance, 2025 (Ordinance No. 25 of 2025)**, gazetted 21 May 2025.
- **Cyber Security Bill, 2026:** Parliament passed it on 10 Apr 2026, converting the Ordinance into the **Cyber Security Act, 2026**.
- **Cyber Security (Amendment) Bill, 2026:** placed 27 Jun 2026 and passed around 30 Jun 2026. Reports **conflict on what the passed amendment contains**:
  - bdnews24 reports the passed amendment only deleted s.20 (online gambling).
  - Bangladesh Post and Cyberdelegate report the government's stated aims: faster takedown of "harmful and defamatory AI-generated content", deadlines for platforms such as Meta, new definitions of misinformation and defamation, and BTRC powers to block content and demand user data.
  - Further draft changes were reported in Sep 2026, including possible online-defamation and rumour offences of up to 5 and 10 years. These are **not enacted** as far as found.

**Key points:**
- **Removed in 2025:** the Ordinance annulled nine CSA-2023 sections (21, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 31, 34). These covered defamation, the "identity fraud/impersonation" offence (s.24), hurting religious sentiment, and offensive or false content. Pending cases under them were dropped (Daily Star, 22 May 2025).
- **Added in 2025:** offences for AI-enabled crime. These include using or spreading AI-generated content for blackmail, sextortion, sexual harassment, revenge porn or child sexual abuse material, and AI attacks on critical information infrastructure. Cyber-terrorism includes impersonation by forging identity documents.
- **Penalties:** up to 10 years and Tk 1 crore depending on the offence. Company owners, directors and officers can be liable for company offences. The law applies extraterritorially.
- **Relevance to Motion Studio:** voice clones and digital twins misused for sexual or blackmail content fall directly within these offences.
- **Open question:** whether a general defamation or impersonation offence has returned in 2026 must be checked in the Gazette text. Civil and penal defamation (Penal Code ss.499–500) still exist regardless.

**Sources:**

- https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/97045f1ac1ec
- https://bangladeshpost.net/posts/cyber-security-amendment-bill-placed-in-js-170509
- https://thehonourable.org/same-cage-new-name-bangladeshs-cyber-laws-and-the-limits-of-online-speech/

**Confidence:** **high** for the 2025 repeal and its content. **medium** that a 2026 Act exists. **low** on the contents of the June 2026 amendment.

### 3. Payments: Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2024 and the wallet question

**Status:**
- **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2024:** passed by Parliament July 2024. In force.
- **Bangladesh Bank "Guidelines for issuing Prepaid Instrument (PI) by non-payment system entities, 2024" (v1.0, June 2024):** in force as far as found. No replacement was found.
- **Draft E-Money Issuer regulations (Nov 2025):** these would license bank EMIs and non-bank "DEMIs" (Tk 50 crore capital). Final status is **not confirmed**.

**Key points:**
- **The Act:** Bangladesh Bank approval is needed to **issue, buy or sell a "Prepaid Payment Instrument"** and to run payment services. Operating without approval carries up to 5 years and/or Tk 50 lakh (FE, 2 Jul 2024).
- **The 2024 PI Guidelines** directly cover **closed PIs**: stored value in BDT, bought with money, usable **only for the issuer's own goods or services** (vouchers, gift cards, wallets).
  - **Approval:** the issuer "shall take approval from Bangladesh Bank". **No exemption** for non-cash-out store credit was found.
  - **Eligibility (closed PI):** a registered company with **5 years of business in Bangladesh and 5 years of audited accounts**, and **minimum paid-up capital of Tk 20 crore**.
  - **Limits:**

    | Limit | Rule |
    |---|---|
    | Maximum face value | Tk 10,000 |
    | Individual holding | Tk 1 lakh |
    | Maximum expiry | 1 year (one extension of up to 1 year) |
    | Maximum discount | 20% |
    | Cash-out / transfer | Not allowed |
    | Expired balances | Held 1 year, then deposited with Bangladesh Bank for the government account |

- **Reading of the sources (not advice):** a prepaid NexaTute or Motion Studio wallet sold to Bangladeshi users for Taka looks like a "closed PI". On these sources it would need Bangladesh Bank approval that a newly incorporated company cannot meet.
  - Alternatives a lawyer may wish to test: pay-per-purchase or subscription with no stored balance; non-purchased promotional credits; or packaging credits as a pre-paid service plan rather than stored value. Whether any of these falls outside "PI" is **not confirmed**.
- **Related risk: the aamarPay merchant account.** Bangladesh Bank publicly warned against **22 unlicensed online payment aggregators/platforms** (Dhaka Tribune / Bonik Barta). The DCOG also requires the seller's own trade licence, VAT and TIN details to be displayed. Collecting RemonLogic's sales through **Vitlous Technology's** merchant account may raise questions under the PSA/aggregator rules and the gateway's own terms. **Not researched in depth.**

**Sources:**
- https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/psd/jun062024psd06.pdf
- https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/parliament-passes-payment-and-settlement-system-bill-2024
- https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1279891
- https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/business/420801/bb-warns-against-22-unauthorized-online-payment

**Confidence:** **high** on the PI Guidelines' text. **medium** on whether a SaaS credit balance is a "PI". **low** on whether the e-money regulations have been finalised.

### 4. Digital Commerce Operation Guidelines 2021 (DCOG)

**Status:**
- **DCOG 2021:** issued by the Ministry of Commerce, 4 Jul 2021, with later amendments in 2021. Still the operative instrument as far as found.
- **Digital Business Identity (DBID) Registration Guidelines 2022:** sit under the DCOG.
- **Proposed Digital Commerce Act and Authority:** discussed since 2023. **Not enacted.**
- **Draft "Cross-Border Digital Commerce Policy, 2026":** comments closed 6 Aug 2026. Proposes DBID for foreign platforms, mandatory refund and return disclosure, and cross-border escrow. **Draft.**

**Key points:**
- **Scope:** digital commerce in **goods and services** through websites, apps and social pages. For services, the seller must disclose the delivery method, the technology used and the provider's qualifications (Mahbub & Co). It is not clear on purely digital or SaaS products, but services are expressly covered.
- **Display duties:** trade licence, VAT registration number, TIN, UBID/DBID or PRA. Terms (refund, return, delivery) **in Bangla**, optionally in other languages. Cookie disclosure and checkbox consent before collecting personal data.
- **Delivery:** hand-over within 48h of payment; delivery within 5 days (same city) or 10 days (other city).
- **Refunds:** for delay after advance payment, a **full refund within 10 days through the same payment channel**. If the seller cannot supply, notice within 48h and refund within 72h of that notice.
- **Advance payment:** 100% advance only for ready-to-ship goods, or when an escrow approved by Bangladesh Bank is used.
- **Complaints:** a complaint channel, a resolution message within 72h, and a compliance officer who liaises with the Directorate of National Consumer Rights Protection (DNCRP). Reviews cannot be deleted. Records must be kept for 6 years.
- **Bangladesh Bank e-commerce settlement circular (30 Jun 2021):**
  - Category 1 (includes **education fees** and other immediate services) and category 2 (sellers of their own goods or services) settle normally within 5 or 7 days.
  - Other sellers' funds are held by the PSP until delivery is verified.
  - **Vouchers:** payment is withheld until the service is actually provided.
  - Refunds and chargebacks go back through the same payment medium.

**Sources:**
- https://www.thedailystar.net/law-our-rights/news/overview-the-digital-commerce-operation-guidelines-2021-2128871
- https://mahbub-law.com/ministry-of-commerce-issues-detailed-guidelines-on-e-commerce-business
- https://mahbub-law.com/bangladesh-bank-issues-guidelines-on-settling-payments-in-e-commerce-transactions
- https://observerbd.com/news/586154

**Confidence:** **medium** (secondary sources; the official DCOG text was not read). Whether the DCOG binds a pure SaaS provider is **not confirmed**.

### 5. Consumers' Right Protection Act, 2009 (Act No. 26 of 2009)

**Status:** **In force.** No 2025 or 2026 amendment was found (FAOLEX lists it as not amended). Reform has been discussed but not enacted.

**Key points (from general knowledge of the Act; section numbers must be checked):**
- Offences relevant to an online service include:
  - false or misleading advertisement to deceive buyers (s.44);
  - not supplying the goods or services promised for the price (s.45);
  - deceiving by weight or measure, or selling above a fixed price;
  - negligence or irresponsible service that harms life or safety (s.53).
- **Process:** complaints go to the DNCRP (Directorate of National Consumer Rights Protection). The complaint must be made within 30 days of the cause (s.60). The Directorate can impose administrative fines, and part of the fine may go to the complainant.
- The DCOG and the PI Guidelines both route unsupplied-service disputes to this Act. This makes service-delivery and refund promises in NexaTute and Motion Studio terms enforceable through the DNCRP.

**Sources:**
- https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/bd/national-legislation/consumers-right-protection-act-2009-act-no-26-2009
- http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1014.html (official text; the link was not opened in this research)

**Confidence:** **medium** that it is in force. **low** on the exact section numbers.

### 6. VAT: VAT and Supplementary Duty Act, 2012

**Status:** **In force**, as amended by the Finance Act 2026 (FY2026-27) and by a VAT and SD (Amendment) Bill, 2026 passed 10 Apr 2026. One source gives the Finance Act 2026 as Act No. 96 of 2026 (**unverified**).

**Key points:**
- **Rates:**
  - The standard rate is **15%**. Reduced rates of 1.5% to 10% apply to listed items.
  - **ITES** (software development, web, hosting, digital content, animation, data processing and others) carries a **5%** rate, and 5% VDS ("Information technology enabled service").
  - **"Television and online program producer"** carries **15%** VDS. This may matter for Motion Studio's video production.
  - The **VAT rate for online education / e-learning could NOT be confirmed.** No exemption was found. It may default to 15%, or 5% if treated as ITES. This needs a lawyer or VAT consultant.
- **Registration:**
  - Mandatory above **Tk 50 lakh** turnover. Turnover tax is 4% between Tk 30 and 50 lakh.
  - Exporters must register regardless of turnover. Exports of services are **zero-rated**, which is relevant to Paddle and other international sales.
- **Imported services (reverse charge):**
  - **15%** VAT on services imported into Bangladesh, such as foreign server or cloud bills.
  - The bank or authorised dealer deposits it by a-challan when the payment is remitted. The challan is the tax invoice, and input credit is available where the service is an input.
  - The Finance Act 2026 rewrote **s.20** (effective 1 Jul 2026, per Lookuptax).
  - Banks also collect 15% VAT on international card payments for some online services, such as online ads (EBL notice).
- **Proposed startup relief:**
  - The FY2026-27 budget proposed **0% VAT for qualifying startups/technology businesses** on local supply, **imported services** and premises rent, **until 30 Jun 2035**. It also proposed full VAT exemption for freelancers and content creators.
  - One industry summary says this was enacted in the Finance Act 2026. The **eligibility definition** (e.g. ICT Division startup certification) was **not confirmed**.
- **Mushak forms:**
  - Mushak-6.3 is the tax invoice, issued at the time of supply.
  - Mushak-6.1 and 6.2 are the purchase and sales books.
  - Mushak-6.6 is the VDS certificate. Mushak-6.7 and 6.8 are credit and debit notes.
  - Mushak-9.1 is the monthly return.
  - VDS applies when the buyer is a limited company, bank, educational institution or similar.

**Sources:**
- https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bangladesh/corporate/other-taxes (reviewed 31 Jul 2026)
- https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/bangladesh/imported-services-reverse-charge-2026
- https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/412346/budget-proposes-major-vat-relief-for-startups

**Confidence:** **high** on the standard rate and the reverse charge. **medium** on the 5% ITES rate. **low** on the online-education rate and on the startup exemption.

### 7. Income tax: Income Tax Act, 2023

**Status:** **In force** (FY2026-27 rates from the Finance Act 2026).

**Key points:**
- **ITES exemption (Sixth Schedule, as amended by the Finance Act 2024):**
  - Income from 20 listed ITES businesses is exempt **from 1 Jul 2024 to 30 Jun 2027**.
  - The list includes **"Software as a service (SaaS)"**, **"E-learning platform and e-publication"**, AI-based solution development, digital animation and others.
  - Conditions:
    - file returns regularly;
    - a yearly tax exemption certificate from the tax office;
    - BASIS membership may be needed.
  - Whether a **cashless-transaction condition** was added for 2025-27 was **not confirmed**.
  - **Expiry 30 Jun 2027** means at most about one tax year of benefit for a company incorporated in Nov 2026, unless extended.
- **Other FY2026-27 measures:**
  - 0% income tax for startups in their growth period (the "Startup Sandbox"; **conditions not confirmed**).
  - 0% for all freelancing and content-creation income (mainly individuals).
- **Corporate rate (non-listed company):** **27.5%**, or **25%** if all transactions go through bank transfer (AY 2026/27 onward, PwC).
- **Withholding on payments to non-residents (s.163 table, PwC):**

  | Payment | Rate |
  |---|---|
  | Technical services or know-how (non-individual) | 10% |
  | Royalty, licence fees, intangibles (likely includes software licences) | 20% |
  | Advertisement making or digital marketing | 10% |
  | Advertisement broadcasting | 15% |
  | Any other payment (possibly cloud/hosting, unless classed as rental or technical) | 20% |

  Treaty rates (the US and Netherlands have DTAAs with Bangladesh) or an NBR certificate can reduce them. How cloud subscriptions are treated is **not confirmed**.

**Sources:**
- https://basis.org.bd/public/files/gazette/fd7cb610579e8e481f70e2934cb011d6-24092024095808.pdf
- https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bangladesh/corporate/withholding-taxes
- https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bangladesh/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income

**Confidence:** **high** on the 2024–2027 exemption and on the corporate rates. **medium** on the WHT table. **low** on the FY27 startup and cashless conditions.

### 8. Foreign exchange

**Status:** Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947 plus Bangladesh Bank's GFET. The current consolidated rules are:
- **FEPD-1 Circular No. 40 (30 Sep 2026):** master circular on outward remittances for current-account items. Valid for 1 year.
- **FEPD-1 Circular No. 22 (22 Jul 2026):** freelancers and individual service exporters.
- **FE Circular No. 31 (31 Jul 2025):** OPGSP, MFS and PSP repatriation channels, and ERQ.

**Key points:**
- **Receiving foreign payments (exports):**
  - Service-export proceeds must be repatriated within the same time limits as goods exports. Keeping proceeds **abroad** in any form (foreign accounts, wallets, crypto) breaches FERA 1947, except approved notional or merchant accounts.
  - Inward remittances above USD 20,000 need online Form-C (ICT).
  - ERQ (Exporters' Retention Quota) allows up to **50%** of net earnings for software and ICT services to be kept in foreign currency. ERQ may be used for software, domain, hosting and server costs.
  - Small proceeds may come through approved OPGSPs (maximum USD 10,000 per transaction).
  - Paddle payouts to a Bangladeshi company bank account would be export receipts. How Paddle-specific payouts are handled was **not confirmed**.
- **Paying foreign server and software bills:**
  - Circular 40, Part-E para 11, lets authorised dealers remit **software maintenance and support, cloud, IT infrastructure and remote application** fees with **no stated annual cap**. Conditions:
    - a licensed product and a valid agreement with the foreign provider;
    - an invoice for each payment;
    - an undertaking to pay through one AD only and to bring funds back if the service is not received;
    - tax and VAT at source;
    - for later payments, a certificate of satisfactory use.
  - Individual developers using virtual cards are capped at USD 500 a year.
  - Corporate international-card rules (Part-J) were **not read**.

**Sources:**
- https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/sep302026fepd-140e.pdf
- https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/jul222026fepd-122e.pdf

**Confidence:** **high** on what the circulars say. **medium** on how they apply to a Paddle merchant-of-record payout.

### 9. Copyright Act, 2023

**Status:** **In force.** It replaced the Copyright Act 2000. The Act number was not confirmed in this research.

**Key points:**
- The Act does **not expressly address AI or non-human authorship**. AI-generated works may not get the same protection (Chambers 2026; Daily Star, 7 Jun 2026).
- Copyright arises automatically. Registration with the Copyright Office is optional but helps enforcement.
- Works made by an employee in the course of employment, or designated as such by agreement, belong to the employer.
- No text-and-data-mining or AI-training exception was reported.
- Licences and assignments should be in writing (standard practice; the exact section was not checked).
- **Relevance:** Motion Studio's terms cannot rely on Bangladeshi law to give users copyright in purely AI-generated output. Rights in voices and likeness come from consent and contract, not copyright.

**Sources:**
- https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/Trade-Marks-and-Copyright-2026/37/

**Confidence:** **medium**.

### 10. Children and minors

**Status:** all **in force**.
- **Children Act, 2013** (Act No. 24 of 2013): a child is under 18 (s.4).
- **Majority Act, 1875:** majority at 18.
- **Contract Act, 1872, s.11:** minors cannot contract. A minor's agreement is void (*Mohori Bibee*).
- **PDPA 2026:** child under 18 and guardian consent (see item 1).

**Key points:**
- Contracts and payments for under-18 users should be made by the parent or guardian. A contract with a minor is void and cannot be enforced against the minor.
- The Children Act 2013 mainly governs child protection and justice. No specific online-service duty was found in it. Its protections (for example, no publication identifying a child in conflict with the law) may matter for recordings and showcases.
- The online-specific duties for children's data come from the PDPA (item 1): guardian consent, and no profiling, tracking or targeted ads.
- No Bangladeshi rule specifically on minors' **online** contracts was found.

**Sources:**
- https://www.supremecourt.gov.bd/resources/contents/Children_Act_2013-Brief_Commentary_v4.pdf
- https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/

**Confidence:** **high** (age 18, void minor contracts). **medium** (no other online rule).

### 11. Companies Act, 1994: the objects clause

**Status:** **In force.** No change was found that abolishes objects clauses.

**Key points:**
- The Memorandum must state the company's objects. The company is confined to them (ultra vires doctrine).
- **Changing the objects** needs a special resolution **and High Court Division confirmation** under ss.12–13. Example: *Plastic Touch Industries Ltd*, Company Matter 1544/2025, confirmed 14 Oct 2025. The judgment was conditioned on a Tk 50,000 donation, then filing with the RJSC within 90 days.
- In practice, Bangladeshi MoAs list detailed and broad objects with ancillary powers. Commentators argue for reform, but s.12 still stands.
- **Implication from the sources (not advice):** draft the objects at incorporation (1 Nov 2026) widely enough to cover:
  - AI tutoring and education;
  - SaaS and software;
  - AI video, voice and avatar production;
  - payments and wallet-related services (subject to item 3);
  - export of ITES;
  - data processing;
  - marketing.

  This avoids a later court application.

**Sources:**
- https://supremecourt.gov.bd/resources/documents/1_CompanyMatter1544of2025.pdf
- https://lawhelpbd.com/business/company-law/altering-memorandum-association/

**Confidence:** **high** on the court-confirmation requirement. **medium** on current RJSC practice.

## International

### 12. GDPR Article 27 (EU) and UK GDPR Article 27

**Status:** **In force.** The UK GDPR Art. 27 is still in place after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Only the regulator's name changed on 30 Sep 2026 (S.I. 2026/386).

**Key points:**
- **When a representative is required:** a non-EU (or non-UK) controller or processor caught by Art. 3(2) must designate **in writing** a representative in an EU member state where the data subjects are (or in the UK). Art. 3(2) catches anyone who offers goods or services to people there, or monitors their behaviour there.
- **Exemption:** processing that is **occasional**, does **not** include large-scale special-category or criminal data, and is unlikely to create risk. Public bodies are also exempt.
- **Why the exemption is unlikely to help:**
  - A SaaS marketed to EU or UK users on a continuing basis is not "occasional".
  - Voiceprints and face data used for identification are special-category (biometric) data under Art. 9.
  - Children's data raises the risk.
- **The representative's role:** acts as a contact for regulators and data subjects. The controller remains liable.
- **Selling via Paddle as merchant of record does not remove this.** RemonLogic still controls the user data on its own platform.

**Sources:**
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj (Art. 27, Art. 3(2), Art. 9)
- https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/27/data.html

**Confidence:** **high**.

### 13. EU AI Act Article 50 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

**Status:**
- **Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026.**
- The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) delayed the high-risk rules to Dec 2027 but **kept the Art. 50 date**.
- The only relief is a **grace period until 2 December 2026** for the **50(2) marking duty**. It covers only generative systems placed on the market **before** 2 Aug 2026.
- The Commission adopted Art. 50 guidelines on 20 Jul 2026. A voluntary **Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content** was published 31 Jul 2026.

**Key points:**
- **Providers:**
  - **50(1):** AI systems that interact with people (for example, the NexaTute AI tutor) must tell users they are dealing with AI from the first interaction, unless that is obvious.
  - **50(2):** generative systems producing audio, image, video or text (Motion Studio) must **mark outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way**. The marking must be effective, robust and interoperable (C2PA or watermarking, for example).
- **Deployers:**
  - **50(4):** whoever publishes **deepfakes** (realistic content resembling real people, places or events) must **clearly label them visibly**, at the latest on first exposure.
  - For evidently artistic, satirical or fictional works, the disclosure may be light so it does not spoil the work.
  - AI-generated text on matters of public interest must be disclosed unless it was human-edited under editorial responsibility.
  - The provider's hidden watermark alone does not satisfy the deployer's duty.
  - Motion Studio users who publish are deployers. Motion Studio may be both provider and deployer when it publishes for them.
- **Who it covers:** providers and deployers outside the EU whose output is used in the EU.
- **Fines:** up to €15m or 3% of worldwide turnover.
- **Timing for this company:** a product launched after 2 Aug 2026 gets no grace period for marking.

**Sources:**
- https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
- https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live

**Confidence:** **high**.

### 14. CCPA/CPRA and COPPA (USA)

**Status:** both **in force**.

**Key points:**
- **CCPA/CPRA** covers a for-profit business that "does business in California", which can include online sales or tracking of California residents without a physical presence. It applies if the business meets **any one** of these:
  - annual gross revenue above **$26,625,000** (from 1 Jan 2025; adjusted in odd years, so 2027 may change). This counts worldwide revenue, not only California revenue.
  - buys, sells or shares the personal information of **100,000+** California consumers or households a year;
  - gets **50%+** of revenue from selling or sharing personal information.

  A small foreign SaaS below all three thresholds is **not covered**. New regulations effective 1 Jan 2026 (risk assessments, ADMT, cybersecurity audits) apply only to covered businesses.
- **COPPA:**
  - Applies to operators of child-directed services, or those with **actual knowledge** of collecting data from **under-13s in the US**, whatever the operator's country.
  - The **amended COPPA Rule** took effect 23 Jun 2025, with **full compliance required from 22 Apr 2026**. It requires:
    - **separate** verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties for non-integral purposes, which the summaries say includes targeted ads and **AI model training**;
    - a **written retention policy** in the privacy notice, with indefinite retention prohibited;
    - a **written information-security programme**;
    - written assurances from vendors.
  - **Biometric identifiers (voiceprints, faceprints) are now "personal information".**
  - Penalties are up to **$53,088 per violation**.
  - This affects NexaTute recordings of US under-13 users.

### 15. DMCA §512 designated agent

**Status:** **In force.** The process is in 37 C.F.R. §201.38.

**Key points:**
- **Who must register:** a service provider that stores user material and wants the §512(c) safe harbour. It must register an agent **online** in the Copyright Office DMCA Designated Agent Directory and publish the same details on its website.
- **Fee:** **$6** per designation, amendment or resubmission.
- **Expiry:** a designation **expires after 3 years** unless renewed by amendment or resubmission ($6).
- **The agent** may be a person, a role, a department or a third party (a law firm or takedown service). Only one agent per provider.
- **P.O. box:** allowed for the provider's address only with prior Office approval in exceptional cases. The agent's address does not need to be a P.O. box.
- **Foreign providers:** the Copyright Office FAQ does **not expressly say** whether they can register. The rule covers any "service provider" under §512(k)(1), which is not limited to US entities. In practice foreign companies do register, but this is **not confirmed from an official statement**.

**Sources:**
- https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/
- https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html

**Confidence:** **high** (fee, expiry, process). **medium** (foreign eligibility).

### 16. US digital-replica and voice laws

**Status:**
- **Tennessee ELVIS Act:** in force since 1 Jul 2024.
- **California AB 2602 and AB 1836:** in force since 1 Jan 2025 (from general knowledge; not checked in a primary source here).
- **Federal NO FAKES Act of 2026** (S.4591 / H.R.8915, introduced 20 May 2026): passed the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously in June 2026. **Not enacted** as of late Oct 2026.

**What each one does:**
- **ELVIS Act:** extends Tennessee's publicity right to **voice**, including AI simulations. It creates liability for publishing or distributing unauthorised voice or likeness content, and for making available tools or algorithms whose purpose is to produce someone's voice or likeness without authorisation.
- **California AB 2602** (Labor Code §927): makes contract terms that allow a **digital replica** to replace a performer's work unenforceable, unless the use is reasonably specifically described and the performer had legal or union representation.
- **California AB 1836** (Civ. Code §3344.1): creates liability for digital replicas of **deceased** personalities without estate consent.
- **NO FAKES Act (if enacted):** would create a federal, licensable right in voice and visual likeness. It would add a notice-and-takedown duty for online services and a DMCA-style counter-notice.

**Relevance to Motion Studio:** consent, scope and licence records for every cloned voice and digital twin are the core control under these laws. Other states (NY, IL and others) have related laws that were not researched.

### 17. Platform rules

**Status:** these are current platform policies. They can change without notice.

**YouTube:**
- **Disclosure:** creators must disclose **realistic** content that is AI-generated or altered. Examples:
  - a real person saying or doing something they didn't;
  - altered real events or places;
  - realistic scenes that never happened;
  - AI music that is the main focus.

  Disclosure is the "AI use" toggle in YouTube Studio.
- **Exempt:** unrealistic or animated content, beauty filters, captions, and **cloning one's own voice for voiceover**.
- **Labels:** YouTube may auto-label (C2PA metadata, its own tools, detection). Repeated non-disclosure can lead to a forced label, removal, or **YPP suspension**. Disclosure itself does not reduce monetisation.
- **YPP "inauthentic content":** formerly "repetitious content", renamed 15 Jul 2025. **Mass-produced or repetitive** videos are not monetisable, for example template narrations that differ only superficially. Using AI is **not** itself a violation.
  - **This is a direct risk** to Motion Studio's bulk or templated Islamic and story content published for monetising clients.

**Meta (Facebook/Instagram):**
- Shows an "**AI info**" label when it detects industry signals (C2PA/IPTC) or the user self-discloses.
- Users are expected to use the AI disclosure tool for photorealistic video or realistic audio (secondary sources). Meta's own newsroom does not set out a penalty.
- A more prominent label applies to high-risk deceptive content. Political and social-issue ads must disclose digitally altered realistic media.

**TikTok:**
- Requires the "**AI-generated content**" label on content that realistically depicts people, places or events generated or significantly altered by AI.
- Auto-labels uploads with C2PA metadata.
- Unlabelled realistic AI content may be labelled, have its reach reduced, or be removed. Branded content needs both the AIGC label and the commercial-content toggle.
- TikTok's help page could not be fetched, so this is from secondary sources.

**Sources:**
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-clarifies-july-15-policy-update-repetitious-content-rebranded-as-inauthentic-content/
- https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/
- https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements

**Confidence:** **high** for YouTube (official page). **medium** for Meta and TikTok.
