এই document-এ RASP-এর পূর্ববর্তী feature proposals পুনরায় audit করে একটি
consolidated specification তৈরি করা হয়েছে। RASP-কে শুধু AI answer
correction utility হিসেবে নয়, RemonLogic-এর shared AI quality, safety,
evidence, governance and operations layer হিসেবে ধরা হয়েছে। একটি shared
core থাকবে; কিন্তু প্রতিটি product-এর data, permissions, validators, risk
policies ও review workflows আলাদা থাকবে।

-   Core pattern: Generate → Validate → Score/Risk → Correct/Retry →
    Re-validate → Release/Abstain/Review → Record.

-   Critical design principle: model-generated confidence বা model
    agreement একা correctness-এর proof নয়.

-   High-risk actions must be gated by deterministic authorization and
    explicit policy; the model cannot grant itself permissions.

-   Knowledge/correction memory must be governed, scoped, versioned,
    auditable, reversible and deletion-aware.

-   Production readiness requires measurable acceptance criteria, test
    evidence, owners, incident procedures and rollback—not merely a
    complete feature list.

Key Deep-Review Findings

-   Earlier proposals covered most functional feature categories, but
    several operational controls needed explicit specification:
    threat-model test coverage, server-side authorization, tenant
    isolation testing, deletion propagation, multimodal evaluation,
    idempotency, release-bundle compatibility, evaluation-data
    contamination controls, queue/capacity protection and human-review
    QA.

-   Some features overlapped and should be consolidated into shared
    modules rather than implemented as separate systems.

-   RASP must be risk-based: low-risk tasks can use lightweight checks;
    consequential or irreversible actions require stronger evidence,
    human approval or safe abstention.

-   Framework alignment is a design aid; it does not establish legal
    compliance or certification.

1. Goals, Non-Goals & Design Principles

1.1 Goals

-   Reduce factual, reasoning, calculation, policy, code and
    media-quality errors before delivery.

-   Detect unsupported claims, contradictory output, stale evidence and
    requirement violations.

-   Correct outputs through bounded, observable retries and independent
    evidence/tools.

-   Safely abstain, disclose uncertainty or escalate when verification
    is insufficient.

-   Protect users, tenants, data, tools, production systems and
    model/provider integrations.

-   Measure quality, cost, latency, incidents and correction
    effectiveness over time.

-   Support shared reliability capabilities without merging
    product-specific knowledge or permissions.

1.2 Non-goals / explicit limitations

-   RASP cannot guarantee zero errors or absolute truth.

-   Self-critique, model consensus or confidence scores are not
    sufficient proof of correctness.

-   Correction memory/RAG is not equivalent to model-weight training or
    permanent learning.

-   Automated validators cannot replace qualified human review for every
    high-impact or context-sensitive decision.

-   Framework mapping does not automatically mean certification,
    regulatory compliance or legal approval.

1.3 Design principles

-   Fail safely for high-risk actions; degrade gracefully for low-risk
    informational tasks.

-   Least privilege, tenant isolation, data minimization and privacy by
    design.

-   Independent verification where practical; deterministic tools for
    calculable/structured claims.

-   Evidence and provenance attached to decisions; no fabricated
    citations or evidence.

-   Version everything that can affect output: model, prompt, tools,
    policy, source/index, validator and workflow.

-   Human accountability for consequential decisions, publication and
    production deployment.

-   Measure false acceptance and false rejection, not only aggregate
    accuracy.

2. Target Architecture

Logical architecture (implementation may be distributed):

1.  Product entry points: NexaTute, Remon Motion Studio, NexaLogicDev;
    each passes tenant, user, product, task type and policy context.

2.  Identity & Policy Gateway: authenticate user/service, authorize
    requested action, enforce product/tenant boundaries and policy
    version.

3.  Task Orchestrator: classify task/risk, select workflow, apply
    latency/cost/retry budgets and route to approved models/tools.

4.  Generation Layer: model/provider/local inference or product-specific
    generation service.

5.  Evidence & Retrieval Layer: approved source registry, scoped
    retrieval, freshness/provenance and access controls.

6.  Verification Layer: independent validators, deterministic tools,
    task-specific quality checks and adversarial checks.

7.  Decision Gate: release, correct/retry, disclose limitations,
    abstain, escalate or block action.

8.  Human Review Workbench: reviewer assignment, evidence, diffs,
    approval, appeal and audit.

9.  Reliability Data Plane: versioned correction registry, test
    datasets, event/audit store, metrics and incident records.

10. Operations Plane: monitoring, SLOs, capacity controls, incident
    response, disaster recovery, release management and rollback.

2.1 Shared core vs product plugins

2.2 Data isolation rules

-   Separate product and tenant namespaces for retrieval indexes,
    correction records, caches, logs, files and tool credentials.

-   Cross-product sharing limited to approved generic rules, validator
    code, anonymized metrics and reusable test methods.

-   Never transfer private prompts, student records, customer assets,
    proprietary code or project-specific corrections across products
    without explicit authorization and lawful basis.

-   Every retrieval/tool operation must re-check authorization at
    execution time; prior conversation context is not permission.

-   Deletion/update requests must propagate to derived indexes/caches
    where applicable, with backup retention exceptions documented.

3. Consolidated Feature Modules

3.1 Verification & Self-Correction Engine

-   Claim-level verification for factual statements and critical
    assertions.

-   Independent verification strategy: alternate source, deterministic
    tool, separate verifier or different method where appropriate.

-   Contradiction, unsupported-claim, hallucinated-citation and
    instruction-compliance checks.

-   Math/science checks using symbolic/numeric tools, units and domain
    constraints.

-   Bounded retry/correction loop with retry, time, token, tool and cost
    budgets.

-   Failure policy: release with caveat, abstain, safe fallback, human
    review or block.

-   Correction diff and concise explanation of what changed, where
    possible.

-   Quality scorecard with calibrated metrics; avoid presenting score as
    guarantee.

3.2 Evidence, Knowledge & Decision Integrity

-   Source trust registry: authority, jurisdiction, date, version,
    license, access rights and freshness.

-   Evidence traceability from claim/output to source/tool/test
    evidence.

-   Knowledge ingestion validation, malware/content screening,
    quarantine and approval.

-   Data poisoning and prompt-injection screening for retrieved content
    and datasets.

-   Correction registry with scope, author, reviewer, validity, expiry,
    provenance and rollback.

-   Approved requirement/decision registry with authority hierarchy and
    conflict detection.

-   Change-impact analysis when requirements, source editions, policies
    or models change.

-   Lineage and deletion propagation across documents, embeddings,
    caches, derived artifacts and correction records.

3.3 AI Security & Agent Control Plane

-   Threat model and attack test suite mapped to applicable OWASP GenAI
    risks.

-   Server-side authorization and policy decision point for every
    tool/action.

-   Prompt-injection defenses for user input, retrieved documents, code
    comments, media metadata and tool outputs.

-   Least privilege, tool allowlists, scoped credentials, sandboxing and
    network egress controls.

-   Sensitive information/secret detection and redaction; secure
    logging.

-   Excessive agency controls: action limits, approval gates, loop
    guards and execution budgets.

-   Output handling validation before HTML, SQL, shell, code, API or
    downstream system use.

-   Vector/embedding access controls, tenant filters, integrity checks
    and retrieval attack testing.

-   Security red-team exercises, vulnerability disclosure and
    remediation workflow.

3.4 Model, Provider & Release Governance

-   Model/provider registry with capabilities, limitations, version,
    region, data terms, license and approval status.

-   Model/prompt/tool/policy/source-index/validator version binding in
    release manifest.

-   Shadow testing, canary rollout, A/B evaluation and automatic/manual
    rollback.

-   Provider outage, deprecation, terms change and exit/migration plan.

-   Model drift, behavior change and quality regression detection.

-   Release readiness gates based on risk, test coverage, security and
    SLOs.

-   Supply-chain provenance for models, packages, datasets, containers
    and generated artifacts.

3.5 Evaluation, Benchmark & Quality Analytics

-   Golden datasets with ownership, versioning, representativeness and
    privacy review.

-   Dataset contamination/leakage controls; holdout and adversarial test
    sets.

-   Task-specific metrics: factuality, groundedness, reasoning, code
    test pass, media quality, safety, latency and cost.

-   False-acceptance and false-rejection rates; confidence calibration
    and uncertainty evaluation.

-   Regression tests for every approved correction and resolved
    incident.

-   Bias/fairness evaluation across language, locale, accessibility and
    relevant user groups.

-   Coverage map: identify output types, risks and products lacking
    validators/tests.

-   Quality SLOs and release thresholds defined per product/task/risk.

3.6 Human Review, Appeals & Accountability

-   Role-based reviewer permissions, assignment, SLA, escalation and
    workload limits.

-   Double review or specialist review for designated critical cases.

-   Reviewer calibration, disagreement resolution, audit and quality
    sampling.

-   User/teacher/customer appeal and dispute workflow with status and
    resolution record.

-   Approval expiry/re-review triggers when source, model, policy or
    output changes.

-   Human approval for consequential decisions, public release of
    sensitive content and production deployment.

-   Clear separation between AI suggestion, reviewer decision and
    authorized action.

3.7 Reliability Operations & Resilience

-   Observability: quality, error classes, latency, cost, retries,
    fallbacks, queue depth and validator health.

-   Incident lifecycle: detect, classify, contain, investigate, correct,
    communicate and prevent recurrence.

-   Root-cause analysis and duplicate-error clustering.

-   Capacity controls: per-tenant quotas, concurrency, backpressure,
    priority queues and overload mode.

-   Idempotency keys and workflow state management for retries,
    duplicate delivery and partial failures.

-   Disaster recovery: backups, restore tests, RTO/RPO targets, failover
    and continuity runbooks.

-   Kill switch, safe mode, feature flags and rollback controls.

-   Privacy-safe logs, retention/deletion, access audit and export
    controls.

3.8 Transparency, Accessibility & User Experience

-   AI-generated/corrected content disclosure where appropriate and
    required.

-   Clear statuses: checking, verified against defined checks, needs
    review, or unable to verify.

-   Explain limitations without implying certainty; accessible language
    and UI.

-   Accessibility testing for captions, screen readers, keyboard
    navigation, contrast and readable explanations.

-   User feedback/report-error flow and controlled conversion into test
    cases.

-   Reliability Passport: model/version, evidence references,
    validators, correction events, approval and limitations—redacted for
    privacy.

4. Product-Specific Requirements

4.1 NexaTute — AI Tutor

-   Curriculum registry by board, syllabus, class/grade, subject, exam
    year and textbook edition.

-   Answer grounding and source edition checks; distinguish official
    source from supplementary explanation.

-   Step-by-step math/science verification, unit checks and independent
    answer-key cross-check.

-   Marking-rubric and exam-answer completeness validation; flag
    ambiguous questions.

-   Misconception detection and pedagogical quality: explain at learner
    level, then check understanding.

-   Teacher override with versioning, approval, scope, expiry and
    rollback.

-   Student privacy, age-appropriate safety, role-based access,
    retention/deletion and recording-consent integration.

-   Learning fairness across languages, proficiency levels and
    accessibility needs.

-   Live classroom latency budget, mic/request-to-speak controls, safe
    fallback and escalation for urgent/sensitive queries.

-   AI must not autonomously make consequential grading/eligibility
    decisions without defined human oversight.

4.2 Remon Motion Studio

-   Creative brief compliance: requested scenes, duration, aspect ratio,
    style, brand rules and deliverables.

-   Script fact-checking and narrative/storyboard continuity graph.

-   Character, location, prop, chronology and visual-style consistency
    checks.

-   Audio, voice, lip-sync, subtitle, timing and audio-video
    synchronization validation.

-   Render integrity: playable file, duration, resolution, frame/audio
    integrity, checksum and delivery verification.

-   Scene-level retry/re-render, retry budget and cost-aware repair.

-   Asset/voice/likeness consent, license, provenance, scope and

-   Content-plan policy enforcement; Islamic content requires configured

4.3 NexaLogicDev

-   Requirements-to-test traceability and architecture rule checks.

-   Sandboxed build/test with resource limits, restricted network egress
    and no default production credentials.

-   Compile/lint/unit/integration/security tests and failure-driven
    patch loop.

-   Dependency, license, secret, vulnerability and supply-chain scans.

-   API/schema/contract compatibility and database migration dry-run.

-   Patch diff, impact summary, test evidence and rollback plan.

-   Signed/provenance-tracked build artifacts and reproducible-build
    checks where feasible.

-   Human authorization for deployment, destructive migrations and
    sensitive changes.

-   Canary deployment, post-deployment monitoring, rollback and
    vulnerability response workflow.

6. Risk Tiers & Decision Policy

Risk tier is contextual. A task can be escalated based on uncertainty,
audience size, reversibility, sensitive data, impact or source conflict.

7. Reliability Passport & Audit Events

For each material output/action, store a privacy-minimized record
containing:

-   Output/task ID; product/tenant reference; timestamp; risk tier;
    policy version.

-   Model/provider/version; prompt/workflow version; tool/validator
    versions.

-   Evidence/source references and freshness metadata—not unnecessary
    raw private content.

-   Validation results, failures, retries, uncertainty decision and
    final disposition.

-   Human reviewer/approval identity and decision where applicable.

-   Artifact checksum/provenance for code/media outputs.

-   Known limitations, incident linkage and rollback/deletion status.

Records must be access-controlled, tamper-evident where feasible,
retention-limited and exportable for authorized audit.

8. Metrics & Acceptance Criteria

Do not use one global 'accuracy' number. Define targets by product, task
type, risk tier and language. Establish baseline first, then approve
targets.

Numeric targets should be set after baseline evaluation and
load/security testing; do not invent universal targets before workload
characterization.
